# PeatPulse opening: carbon context

Research checked on 3 October 2026. The user wants the audience to understand the carbon-emissions problem and Scotland's exposure before introducing the model or inspection shortlist. Figures and screen copy below remain candidates, not approved design decisions.

## Supported facts

- **Scotland, all peatland degradation:** the Scottish Government's Climate Change Plan reports peatland emissions of 6.16 million tonnes CO2e in 2023, representing 16% of Scotland's total emissions. The surrounding discussion concerns degraded peatlands, including drainage and other damage. This percentage must not be described as emissions from peatland fires alone. [Scottish Government, 24 March 2026](https://www.gov.scot/publications/scotlands-climate-change-plan-2026-2040-annexes/pages/14/).
- **UK, wildfire carbon:** research from Cambridge, Exeter and the Met Office covering 2001–2021 finds fires that burned into peat caused up to 90% of annual UK fire-driven carbon emissions, with peaks in particularly dry years. Preserve “up to”, annual, UK, wildfire/fire-driven, and the dry-year context; this is not a share of all UK or Scottish emissions. [University of Exeter, 21 February 2025](https://news.exeter.ac.uk/faculty-of-environment-science-and-economy/dry-years-fuel-uk-peatland-fire-carbon-emissions/).
- **Scottish fire example:** that study's reported estimate for the 2019 Flow Country fire is 96,000 tonnes of carbon from burning peat. This is an estimate of elemental carbon, not 96,000 tonnes of CO2. Estimates from other studies differ with assumptions and mapped burned area. [Exeter study summary](https://news.exeter.ac.uk/faculty-of-environment-science-and-economy/dry-years-fuel-uk-peatland-fire-carbon-emissions/).
- **Carbon store:** NatureScot reports approximately 400 million tonnes of carbon stored across the Flow Country peatland region. This is stored carbon, not annual emissions or an amount expected to burn. [NatureScot, 26 July 2024](https://www.nature.scot/scotlands-flow-country-secures-world-heritage-site-status).

## Proposed narrative

Establish the carbon stored in the landscape; explain that peat burning releases stored carbon; give a properly scoped fire statistic or Scottish example; then introduce prioritising patches for attention. Show one standalone statement at a time, consistent with the user's preference against heading/subheading stacks.

PeatPulse has measured historical ranking results. Carbon savings, fire prevention and operational warning performance have not been demonstrated. Carbon facts provide context rather than measured product impact.

Checked on **3 October 2026**. These are live queries against data publishers, with real rows below. Scene catalogue results establish that imagery exists; we have **not yet extracted satellite pixel values** or measured model accuracy. Counts and latest dates will change.

UKCEH report **55160972**: **21 July 2026**, 56.20048, −3.40761 (Loch Leven area), status **confirmed bloom**; note says UKCEH sampled _Gloeotrichia_ near shore. This is a real example label, although a near-shore report may differ from the lake-wide satellite signal. [Source CSV](https://bloominalgae.ceh.ac.uk/data/BA_records.csv).

Public [Copernicus STAC search](https://stac.dataspace.copernicus.eu/v1/search?collections=sentinel-2-l2a&bbox=-3.42%2C56.18%2C-3.38%2C56.23&datetime=2026-07-18T00%3A00%3A00Z%2F2026-07-24T23%3A59%3A59Z&limit=200) around that point returned scenes on **19, 20 and 22 July**. Their **whole-tile** cloud percentages were **7.76%, 26.38%, 59.91%/76.96%**, respectively. The 19 July scene is two days before the report and has B4/B5 assets. We still need a pixel-level cloud mask over the **lake**, water-edge exclusion, and NDCI calculation before claiming a match.

| Sampling date |                             Result (µg/L) |

The API's `not a bloom` reports are **not** a random set of clean lakes; citizen reports are biased toward suspected events. Chlorophyll is an algae proxy, not a toxin measurement. The [US EPA says satellites cannot detect toxin concentrations](https://www.epa.gov/habs/hab-monitoring-and-trends). A demo can show _bloom likelihood / needs inspection_ and link the original report, without asserting drinking-water safety.

**Smallest credible demo:** pick a lake with reports and sufficiently clear scenes, calculate a cloud-masked B5/B4 NDCI timeline, display report dates and lab chlorophyll alongside it, and label uncertainty.

From [COSMOS-UK Balruddery station](https://cosmos-api.ceh.ac.uk/collections/1D/locations/BALRD?datetime=2026-07-01T00%3A00%3A00Z%2F2026-07-10T00%3A00%3A00Z&parameter-name=cosmos_vwc%2Cprecip%2Cpe%2Cta), 56.482297, −3.111488:

| 1 Jul        |           29.7 |       2.9 |                        1.8 |               13.8 |
| 4 Jul        |           27.2 |       0.0 |                        2.5 |               15.6 |

| 10 Jul       |           22.4 |       0.0 |                        4.6 |               18.6 |

The station declined **7.3 percentage points over nine days**; the 10-day window had **3.2 mm rain** and **31.6 mm potential evaporation**. This is a verified **drying episode**, not enough evidence by itself to classify a flash drought. Inspect data-quality flags and a seasonal baseline before using as a threshold.

The [Sentinel-1 STAC search](https://stac.dataspace.copernicus.eu/v1/search?collections=sentinel-1-grd&bbox=-3.13%2C56.47%2C-3.09%2C56.50&datetime=2026-07-01T00%3A00%3A00Z%2F2026-07-10T23%3A59%3A59Z&limit=200) returned **12 scenes across eight dates** around Balruddery from 1–10 July. A potentially comparable pair is **4 July** and **10 July**: both were VV+VH, descending, relative orbit **154** (different Sentinel-1 spacecraft). These are acquisition records; we have not checked radar backscatter changes yet.

The original “5 days, 3σ = flash drought” rule is an unvalidated project heuristic. [NOAA describes flash drought development on the scale of weeks](https://www.drought.gov/what-is-drought/soil-moisture-and-drought) and points to precipitation and evapotranspiration as context. A safer demo output is _rapid drying / watch area_ until calibrated against a recognised event series.

## What to test next, before picking a concept

1. **ClearRes:** extract actual B4/B5 pixels over Loch Leven on 19 July; compare cloud-masked NDCI with the verified 21 July report and a second lake/date. This resolves the largest image-processing uncertainty.
2. **SoilPulse:** extract Sentinel-1 VV/VH for the Balruddery 4/10 July same-orbit pair; compare with COSMOS soil moisture. Test whether the radar change follows the ground measurement at several sites.
3. **AeroAudit:** test a current VIIRS fire/flare product at the Mariner flare stack, then compare monthly aggregates with NSTA reports. Use methane only as regional context unless wind-aware source attribution works.

For the hackathon, **ClearRes and SoilPulse already have independent field observations for validation**. AeroAudit has unusually good official reporting data, but its original “catch covert venting at one rig” claim is not supported by the available satellite resolution or the regulatory data reviewed here.

### Reproducing the live checks

- NSTA's ArcGIS `FeatureServer/{layer}/query` supports `where=1=1`, `returnCountOnly=true`, `outFields`, `orderByFields`, and `f=json`. The live service URLs are above.
- Copernicus STAC supports unauthenticated scene search by `collections`, `bbox`, `datetime`, and `limit` at `https://stac.dataspace.copernicus.eu/v1/search`.
- UKCEH Bloomin' Algae CSV downloads directly from the link above.
- COSMOS-UK API uses `/collections/1D/locations/{site}?datetime=STARTZ/ENDZ&parameter-name=cosmos_vwc,precip,pe,ta`; full ISO timestamps are required. Its `f=csv` option returns a ZIP containing the CSV.
- Environment Agency observations require **POST** to `/water-quality/data/observation` with URL parameters `dateFrom=2025-01-01&dateTo=2025-12-31&determinand=7887&limit=2500`, headers `Accept: text/csv`, `CSV-Header: present`, `API-Version: 1`, `Content-Type: application/json`, and an empty request body. Filter `samplingPoint.samplingPointType` for lakes/reservoirs and paginate using `skip`.
